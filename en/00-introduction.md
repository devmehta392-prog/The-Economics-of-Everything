# Why This Book Exists

No one teaches you to see the world this way. School gives you formulas — supply curves, GDP, inflation rates — and expects you to somehow connect them to your actual life: the ₹320 you just paid for a coffee, the Netflix plan you keep meaning to cancel, the flight ticket that costs three different prices depending on when you looked.

This book starts from the other direction. Instead of theory looking for an example, it takes eleven things you already run into — a coffee, a cricket match, a sachet of shampoo, a UPI payment — and pulls the economics out of them. Some of it is textbook material wearing ordinary clothes: elasticity, network effects, price discrimination. Some of it is stranger and more human: status, habit, the stories we tell ourselves about what we're buying.

None of these chapters requires you to have taken an economics class. Each one stands on its own, so you can start anywhere — with Starbucks, with Kohli's IPL contract, with the shampoo sachet that somehow makes HUL more money than the bottle does. But read enough of them and a pattern starts to show through: almost nothing is priced the way it looks. The sticker price is rarely the real story.

By the end, the goal isn't for you to recite definitions. It's for you to walk past a Starbucks, or open a food delivery app, or watch a movie ticket price change between Tuesday and Friday, and think: *I know exactly why that's happening.* That instinct — once you have it — doesn't go away.
