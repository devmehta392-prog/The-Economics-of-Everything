# The World Was Pricing You All Along

Every chapter in this book is really the same chapter, told with a different product. A company looks at what you value — status, convenience, belonging, habit, attention — and prices against that, not against its own costs. Starbucks sells identity. Netflix sells routine. Zomato sells time. Instagram sells attention itself, and never mentions the transaction out loud.

Once you notice this, you can't stop noticing it. That's the actual payoff of studying economics this way — not the vocabulary, but the reflex. You'll catch yourself mid-purchase, wondering whether you're paying for the product or the feeling of the product. You'll watch a subscription auto-renew and understand exactly which bias made that easy. You'll see a ₹2 sachet on a shelf and recognise a distribution strategy worth billions.

None of this makes the world less enjoyable. If anything, it makes it more interesting — the same way knowing how a magic trick works doesn't ruin the show, it just lets you watch the magician's hands instead of the coin. You still get to buy the coffee. You just know, now, exactly what you're buying.
